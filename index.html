Cleaning¶

Song Duration¶

The histogram was created using Matplotlib's plt.hist() function with 2,000 bins to visualize the distribution of track lengths across the entire dataset. By converting track duration from milliseconds to minutes (duration_ms / 60000), the plot provides an intuitive look at how song lengths are distributed.

Based on the 'Duration Distribution' graph, the visualization helps identify the common length of songs in the Spotify dataset. Most tracks cluster heavily around the typical 3-to-4-minute mark, creating a sharp central peak. However, because 2000 bins were specified, the distribution reveals fine granularity, including extreme outliers such as ultra-short tracks under a minute and exceptionally long ambient, spoken-word, or classical audio tracks extending well beyond standard song lengths.

The histogram shows how audio track length varies across the dataset. The strong central concentration indicates that most songs adhere to standard commercial track lengths, while the long right tail highlights the presence of non-traditional or genre-specific audio formats. These patterns suggest that while mainstream music heavily favors standard durations, track length alone can vary significantly depending on the specific type of audio content.

Feature Engineering¶

Apart from cleaning the data, we also decided to split the songs into different bins of based off popularity. This will allow us to further investigate audio features across songs with low, medium, and high popularity. We based our cutoffs on the interquartile range to insure we have a proportionate amount of samples in each bin. The box and whisker plot shows Q1, median and Q3 of the data. To make our bins, we split the data from 0 to 30 since that is half way between Q1 and the median. From there we did the same thing for the median and Q3, giving us 30 to 44 as our second bin, leaving 44 to 100 as our final bin.

my_spot['Pop_level'] = pd.cut(
    my_spot['popularity'],
    bins = [-1,30,44,100],
    labels = ['Low', 'Medium', 'High']
)

# Min-Max normalization formula for loudness
my_spot['loudness_scaled'] = (my_spot['loudness'] - my_spot['loudness'].min()) / (
    my_spot['loudness'].max() - my_spot['loudness'].min()
)
# Min-Max normalization formula for tempo
my_spot['tempo_scaled'] = (my_spot['tempo'] - my_spot['tempo'].min()) / (
    my_spot['tempo'].max() - my_spot['tempo'].min()
)
my_spot['average_score'] = my_spot[['danceability', 'loudness_scaled', 'tempo_scaled']].mean(axis=1)

Discussion¶

We can separate these music genres into accoustic (soul, pop, rock) and synthesized (house, electro) music. We create these groups to distinguish the fact that different features correlate with higher popularity. A common factor across the accoustic group is that speechiness correlates positively with popularity. We can infer that the presence of lyrics is important for these genres. Accousticness is also a feature that correlates positively with popularity. This highlights the importance of sound created by instruments rather than synthesized music in these genres. For the synthesized group, we can see that the feature is slightly favored is liveness. This means that the songs that are most popular in these genres are songs that were performed live. One interesting thing to note, is that the electro genre's features do not highly correlate with popularity at all. This could be mean that there are some outside factors that drive this genre to popularity, like cultural trends or the artist who composed the song.

Potential Ethical Concerns in Determining Artist Ratings¶

Far more research would be needed to analyze this, but one possible ethical concern with our analysis could be that the two zero-rated artists we analyzed, Wiz Khalifa and Marvin Gaye, are both Black artists in Black-dominated genres. The fact that they were the only zero-rated artists the group had heard of could mean that either this demographic artists is being rated differently, listeners who enjoy Black artists and genres traditionally dominated by them are being excluded from the dataset, or both. Additionally, one of the top 3 nonzero low-rated artists, Vladimir Nechaev, was nonwestern, meaning some of the same issues could be at play with his rating.

Conclusion¶

This project analyzed a cleaned dataset of 114,000 Spotify tracks to determine which audio features and characteristics best predict a song's popularity. The findings revealed that popularity scores are somewhat normally distributed with an average of 37.8, and that standard commercial track lengths of 3 to 4 minutes heavily dominate the dataset. While factors like explicit content have only a minimal impact on overall success, specific audio profiles yield distinct trends, such as tracks with high "speechiness" consistently clustering alongside lower popularity scores. Ultimately, while individual acoustic features demonstrate complex relationships with track performance, mainstream genres like Electro and Pop, along with established top artists, remain the strongest indicators of high popularity. Artists with very high and very low popularity also have slightly different indicators than the overall dataset and each other, and different models for those indicators had varying levels of effectiveness at explaining variation in popularity.
